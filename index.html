What makes this online chess game unique

Ordinary chess gives you six piece types. Sorcery Chess gives you ten. Alongside the familiar king, queen, rook, bishop, knight and pawn, every side fields four arcane chess pieces: the Wizard poisons an enemy from a distance, the Alchemist cures a cursed ally, the Freezer locks any piece in ice for three moves, and the Witch raises the dead from the graveyard at the cost of her own life. These spellcasters bend the oldest rules of chess without breaking what makes the game feel like chess — turning the most familiar openings into something completely new and unique.

Frequently Asked Questions

Everything newcomers ask about chess, the chess board, and playing Sorcery Chess online — answered.

What is a sorcerer?

A sorcerer is a practitioner of magic — someone who casts spells and bends the rules of the ordinary world. In Sorcery Chess, you play a sorcerer commanding four arcane spellcaster pieces: the Wizard who poisons enemies at range, the Alchemist who cures cursed allies, the Freezer who locks pieces in ice, and the Witch who raises the dead. Each slides like a queen and bends a single rule of chess without breaking the game.

How to play chess?

Chess is a two-player strategy game played on an 8×8 board. White moves first, then players alternate turns. Each piece moves a set way: the rook moves in straight lines, the bishop diagonally, the queen in any direction, the knight in an L-shape, the king one square at a time, and pawns forward one square (capturing diagonally). The goal is checkmate — trapping the enemy king so it cannot escape capture. In Sorcery Chess the same rules apply on a larger 12×12 board, plus four magical spellcaster pieces.

How to set up a chess board?

Place the chess board so each player has a white (light) square in the bottom-right corner. Set the eight pawns along your second row. On the back row, from the corners inward: rook, knight, bishop, then queen and king in the centre — the queen goes on her own colour (white queen on a white square, black queen on a black square) — then bishop, knight, rook. In Sorcery Chess the chess setup is automatic: open the page and the full 12×12 board, including the spellcasters, is arranged for you instantly.

Is chess a sport?

Yes — chess is recognised as a sport by the International Olympic Committee, and its world governing body, FIDE, organises competitive tournaments and world championships. While it is a game of the mind rather than physical exertion, elite chess demands intense concentration, training, stamina and strategy, which is why it is widely classed as a mind sport.

Who invented chess?

Chess was not invented by a single person. It evolved from an ancient Indian game called chaturanga around the 6th century, which spread to Persia (as shatranj) and then through the Islamic world to Europe, where the modern rules — including the powerful queen — took shape by the 15th century. So chess is the work of many cultures over many centuries rather than one inventor.

How many squares are on a chess board?

A standard chess board has 64 squares — an 8×8 grid of 32 light and 32 dark squares. (If you count every square of every size, from 1×1 up to the full 8×8, there are 204 in total.) Sorcery Chess is played on a larger 12×12 board of 144 squares to make room for the classic army plus the four spellcaster pieces on each side.

When was chess invented?

Chess originated around the 6th century AD in India as chaturanga. It reached Persia soon after, spread across the Islamic world, and arrived in Europe by about the 9th–10th century. The modern form of the game, with the moves used today, was established in Europe in the late 15th century.

How to play chess for beginners?

Beginners should start with the basics: learn how each piece moves, set up the board correctly, and understand check (the king is attacked) and checkmate (the king is trapped). A few simple principles go a long way — control the centre, develop your knights and bishops early, keep your king safe, and don't give pieces away for free. The best way to improve is to play often. You can play chess online for free here against five tiers of AI, starting with the gentle Apprentice, and switch to Classic mode for a traditional 8×8 game while you learn.

Piece Guide

WIZARD

Slides like a Queen. Click a blue-glowing enemy on any of its lines to poison it and swap places — it dies after its owner's 2 moves. 4 uses per game, and the spell recharges for 2 of your moves between casts (the ✶ counter shows the wait).

ALCHEMIST

Slides like a Queen. Click a blue-glowing poisoned ally on any of its lines to cure it and swap places with it. Unlimited uses.

WITCH

Slides like a Queen. Revives a fallen piece from the graveyard onto her square — and dies in its place.

❅
FREEZER

Slides like a Queen but never captures. Click a blue-glowing enemy to freeze it for 3 moves, swapping places with it.

HOW TO WIN

Capture the enemy King. Pawns promote to Queen. White ring = capture; blue dashed ring = special ability. Each side gets 5:00 (+2s per move) — run out of time and you lose.
