The Ledger of Renown

Points & Ranks

Every victory is paid in rating points — and not every battlefield pays alike. Here is exactly how renown is earned, how it bleeds, and how it carries you up the seventeen ranks from Junior to The Monarch.

⚖ How a battle pays

Each kind of chess carries its own stakes multiplier. Classic is the baseline; Sorcery's spellcasters make every game harder, so it pays half again as much. The very same multiplier scales the penalty for losing — the richer the reward, the heavier the fall.

🌐 Online duels are friendly exhibitions — they never touch your rating, win or lose.

⚔

Defeats are tracked per game

Conquering the Apprentice in Sorcery Chess does not mark it beaten in Classic. Every chess type keeps its own roll of vanquished foes — so the dread opponents must be earned again on each board you set foot upon.

🔒

The dread foes are earned

The Malevolent stays sealed until you defeat the Apprentice, Sorcerer and Archmage of that variant. The Dominion opens only once all four lesser minds have fallen. They are always timed — and they pay the most.

☠

The price of falling

Through the lower ranks, defeat costs you nothing. But once you rise above the Arcanum (Spellblade and beyond), every loss drains rating — scaled by the variant, exactly like its wins. Glory carries a ransom.

📜

What never scores

Draws pay nothing, and losses below the Arcanum cost nothing. Online matches are unranked. Only the player — always White — earns rating; the computer's victories simply end the duel.

🜔 The seventeen ranks

Points carry you up a fixed ladder. The ranks count down — a newcomer stands at Rank 17, the Monarch reigns at Rank 1. Your current standing glows blue.
