How to play Sorcery Chess

Sorcery Chess keeps everything you already know about chess and adds a layer of magic on top. If you can play ordinary chess, you can play this in minutes — and if you have never played before, this guide teaches you the whole game from the first move to checkmate. Below you will find the board, how every piece moves, the four spellcasters and their spells, the rules for check and checkmate, the clock, and the different ways to win.

1. The goal of the game

Sorcery Chess is a two-player strategy game. You command the white army; your opponent commands the black army. The aim is to defeat the enemy king. You win in one of three ways: by checkmate (trapping the king so it cannot escape capture), by capturing the king directly, or — in a timed game — by flag fall, when your opponent's clock runs out. White always moves first, and then players alternate turns, one move (or one spell) at a time.

2. The board

Standard chess is played on an 8×8 board of 64 squares. Sorcery Chess is played on a larger 12×12 board of 144 squares, which makes room for the classic army plus four extra magical pieces on each side. The board is strictly black and white so nothing distracts from the duel — the only colours you will ever see are the blue glow of magic and the red warning of a king in danger. When you open a game the whole board is set up for you automatically, so there is no fiddly chess setup to learn. Prefer the traditional game? Switch to Classic mode for pure 8×8 chess on a standard board with no spellcasters.

3. How the ordinary pieces move

The six classic chess pieces move exactly as they do in standard chess:

  • Pawn — moves forward one square, or two squares from its starting position, and captures one square diagonally forward. A pawn that reaches the far end of the board promotes to a queen.
  • Rook — slides any number of squares in a straight line, horizontally or vertically.
  • Bishop — slides any number of squares diagonally.
  • Knight — jumps in an L-shape (two squares one way, then one square at a right angle) and is the only piece that can leap over others.
  • Queen — the most powerful classic piece: slides any number of squares in any of the eight directions (straight or diagonal).
  • King — moves one square in any direction. It can never move onto a square where it would be captured, and losing it loses the game.

A piece captures an enemy by moving onto its square. In Sorcery Chess a captured piece is never destroyed — it goes to the graveyard, where it can still be raised again by the Witch (more on her below). There is no castling and no en passant in Sorcery Chess, and pawns promote only to a queen.

4. The four spellcasters

This is what makes Sorcery Chess unique. Alongside the classic army, every side fields four spellcaster pieces. All four move like a queen — sliding any number of squares along any of the eight lines — but each one also carries a single magical ability. A spellcaster's spell can only target the first piece it can see along one of its eight lines, exactly matching the way it moves. When a legal target is available it lights up with a blue glow so you always know what a spell can reach.

  • ♦ Wizard — casts poison. Click a blue-glowing enemy on one of the Wizard's lines to poison it and swap places with it. The poisoned piece dies after its owner has made two more moves. The Wizard has four casts per game, and after each cast it must recharge for two of your own moves before it can cast again.
  • ⚗ Alchemist — casts cure. Click a blue-glowing poisoned ally to remove the poison and swap places with it. The Alchemist has unlimited uses and is your answer to the enemy Wizard.
  • ❅ Freezer — casts frost. Click a blue-glowing enemy to freeze it for three moves, swapping places with it; a frozen piece cannot move or act until the frost thaws — even a queen. The Freezer slides like a queen but never captures, and it must cool down between casts.
  • ⚚ Witch — casts resurrection. The Witch revives one fallen piece from your graveyard onto her own square — and dies in its place. It is a sacrifice, but it can bring back a queen you thought was gone for good. The Witch is used from the side panel, choosing which graveyard piece to raise.

5. Check and checkmate

Your king is in check whenever an enemy piece could capture it on the next move. When you are in check you must get out of it on your turn — by moving the king to safety, blocking the attack, capturing the attacker, or using a spell to neutralise the threat. Freezing or poisoning the checking piece counts as a valid way to escape check, which gives Sorcery Chess defensive options ordinary chess does not have.

If you are in check and there is no legal way to escape, that is checkmate and the game is over. If it is your turn, you are not in check, but you have no legal move at all, the game is a stalemate (a draw). A useful quirk: a frozen piece gives no check while it is frozen, and the Witch's revival never changes which squares are attacked, so it can save you from stalemate but can never itself be a way out of check.

6. The clock

Most Sorcery Chess games are timed with a blitz clock: each side starts with five minutes, and every move you make adds a two-second increment back to your clock. Against the two hardest AI opponents the clock is tighter — three minutes a side. If your time runs out before the game is decided, you lose on time (flag fall). Prefer to think without pressure? You can also choose a clockless game when you start a match, and play entirely at your leisure.

7. Ways to play

There is nothing to download and no account required to start — you can play instantly as a guest. Choose your opponent from the modes screen:

  • Versus the computer — five tiers of AI, from the gentle Apprentice and balanced Sorcerer, through the ruthless Archmage, up to the two dread foes, The Malevolent and The Dominion, which you must unlock by beating the difficulties before them.
  • With a friend — two players sharing one device, passing the screen between moves.
  • Online — challenge a friend on another device. One player creates a room and shares a short code; the other joins with it, and your moves travel directly between your two browsers.

8. Ranks and points

Every victory earns rating points that climb a ladder of seventeen ranks, from Junior all the way to The Monarch. Harder opponents pay more, and your progress saves automatically in your browser. You can read exactly how scoring works on the Points & Ranks page.

Ready to play?

That is the whole game. The fastest way to learn is to play a few relaxed matches against the Apprentice and try each spellcaster once. When you want to sharpen your play, read our Sorcery Chess strategy guide, or study each piece in depth in The Grimoire.
